# Glyphkiln carousel workflow

Use this reference after the skill selects a delivery profile. The live source of
platform limits is `DELIVERY_PROFILE_REGISTRY`; do not duplicate old limits from
memory.

## Sequence blueprint

A sequence is a narrative system, not a quota. Use only the slides the idea
needs, within the selected platform limit.

| Role          | Communication job                                              | Typical visual move                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `hook`        | Make one specific promise, tension, or question.               | Largest type or strongest image; one focal point.      |
| `context`     | Give the minimum framing needed to understand the promise.     | Short setup, label, or before-state.                   |
| `evidence`    | Prove a claim with an attributed example, metric, or artifact. | Oversized fact, crop, comparison, or annotated detail. |
| `explanation` | Turn evidence into one useful idea or step.                    | Diagram, numbered step, or image-plus-caption.         |
| `recap`       | Compress the sequence into a memorable takeaway.               | Repeated motif with reduced detail.                    |
| `action`      | Ask for one relevant next action.                              | Quiet close, direct CTA, and clear endpoint.           |

The first slide normally uses `hook`; the last normally uses `recap` or `action`.
Those are review advisories, not engagement laws. A short sequence may omit
middle roles. A claim-led sequence may repeat `evidence` and `explanation`.

## Composition

- Give each slide one dominant focal point and one primary reading path.
- Preserve a stable grid, type system, palette, numbering system, and motif across
  the deck. Vary scale, alignment, negative space, crop, or evidence treatment so
  the sequence does not become a stack of identical posters.
- Keep labels physically close to the element they explain. Prefer direct labels
  to legends when space allows.
- Use negative space to group and pace information. Empty space should clarify a
  relationship, not merely fill a fixed template.
- Align meaningful content to a small number of shared edges. Break the grid only
  for a deliberate focal shift.
- Design at the actual delivered crop and inspect all slides as thumbnails before
  approving any one slide.

## Typography

- Establish one clear hierarchy: display promise, supporting explanation, then
  metadata. Avoid multiple elements competing at display scale.
- Prefer short, concrete phrasing, but use Glyphkiln's recommended character
  ranges only as editing prompts. Line breaks, fitted size, and phone-size proof
  matter more than word-count folklore.
- Review the exact fitted `fontSize` from render evidence at 360, 390, and 430 px
  delivery widths. Do not judge legibility only on a 1080 px source canvas.
- Use weight, size, spacing, and position together. Do not rely on color alone to
  distinguish a state, category, or chart value.
- Keep line lengths and paragraph shapes visibly intentional. Remove widows,
  awkward single-word lines, fake emphasis, and inconsistent punctuation.

## Visual elements

Every visual element must do at least one job: establish subject, encode evidence,
explain structure, guide attention, or reinforce the campaign identity. Prefer
admitted images, logos, semantic layers, Core-owned shapes, and deterministic
procedural decoration. For charts and diagrams, include redundant labels or
symbols and a source note.

## Avoid

- mixing organic and paid-ad geometry or slide-count advice;
- claiming an official universal TikTok organic safe zone;
- treating 9:16 as the one required TikTok Photo Mode canvas;
- using the same composition on every slide simply for consistency;
- using several focal points, decorative badges, arrows, or shapes with no job;
- placing essential text near a platform overlay edge without device proof;
- rasterizing small body copy, footnotes, or complex tables that cannot survive
  phone-size delivery;
- generic alt text such as “selected image” or captions copied verbatim as alt;
- unattributed statistics, fabricated quotations, or engagement guarantees;
- depending on slide order when an ad surface may reorder slides;
- sending a Core PNG proof to a JPEG-only API path without an explicit,
  documented delivery conversion.

## Final review record

Record the delivery profile ID, profile metadata version, sequence review result,
exact template versions, narrative roles, render fingerprints, approval state,
alt text, source notes, and any device-specific overlay verification. Keep this
record beside the exported artifacts so future platform changes are auditable.
