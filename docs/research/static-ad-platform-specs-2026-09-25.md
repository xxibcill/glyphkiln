# Static ad platform specifications and testing guidance

**Research date:** 2026-09-25  
**Scope:** First-party specifications and creative-testing guidance; no account performance data. This note supports template planning, not renderer qualification.

## What “how many formats?” means

Keep three independent quantities separate:

1. **Export shapes:** aspect ratios and pixel dimensions required by placements.
2. **Creative concepts:** different reasons to buy, evidence, visual subjects, and composition archetypes. Resizing one offer poster is still one concept.
3. **Ads in a test:** the number of independently delivered ads or experiment cells. Asset limits are not recommended experiment sizes.

**Planning recommendation, not an experimentally proven optimum:** Build a reusable library around five shape families: `1:1`, `4:5`, `9:16`, `1.91:1`, and `2:3`. Export only the families needed by the chosen placements. Treat fixed-pixel display banners as a separate delivery family; squeezing a social poster into a leaderboard is not adequate adaptation. The verified platform requirements below justify coverage, not superior conversion performance.

## How many creative variations to test?

- Google recommends **3–4 ads per ad group**, varying images and messages. Its RDA guidance also discourages overlaid logos and text, button graphics, and collages. Consequently, Glyphkiln's complete Product + Offer artwork needs a separate clean-asset export path for responsive ads. [Google RDA guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9823397?hl=en)
- LinkedIn recommends **4–5 variations per campaign**, then using campaign results to refine the creative. This is LinkedIn-specific guidance, not proof that five is optimal on Meta or Pinterest. [LinkedIn campaign guide](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/how-to-advertise-on-linkedin)
- Meta's May 18, 2026 Blueprint course describes diversification across **formats, visuals and messages**. Its publicly readable description does not prescribe a universal number of static templates or testing cells. [Meta Blueprint](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/707402)
- Google's shorter Demand Gen requirements page recommends **3 images per orientation** for horizontal, square and optional `4:5`. This is an asset-supply recommendation; three resized versions are not three independent concepts. [Demand Gen requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17091672?hl=en-GB)

**Working testing recommendation (inference):** Start with three substantially different concepts when budget and traffic support a useful comparison, adapt each to required placements, and measure the business outcome the campaign optimizes for. Use controlled tests for causal comparisons. After selecting promising concepts, test a headline, proof treatment, offer, or visual treatment within them. Do not count every aspect-ratio export as a new creative idea or call an ad a winner because it remains visible in an ad library.

## Important documentation differences

- The RDA creation page lists landscape and square but omits vertical, while the detailed RDA best-practices guide explicitly includes `9:16`. Use the detailed guide as current evidence and verify availability in the advertiser's campaign UI. [RDA creation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7005917?hl=en), [RDA guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9823397?hl=en)
- The shorter Demand Gen requirements page omits `9:16` images; the full creative specifications page explicitly includes them. Do not infer non-support from an abbreviated table. [Requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17091672?hl=en-GB), [Full specifications](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13704860?hl=en)
- Google now says eligible advertisers could begin voluntary Display-to-Demand-Gen migration in June 2026. Future forced migration is described without a date. An export profile should identify the actual campaign type, not assume all accounts have migrated. [RDA guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9823397?hl=en)
- Pinterest converts images to 8-bit RGB JPEG and does not support custom color profiles; it recommends sRGB PNG input. Platform presentation can therefore differ from the local PNG. [Pinterest specifications](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs)

## Scope and confidence

The numerical rows above were read from public first-party pages on the research date. Most pages expose no publication date; TikTok shows September 2026 and Meta Blueprint shows May 18, 2026. Specifications and account eligibility can change. High confidence applies to what the cited page currently states; it does not prove acceptance in every account or comparative campaign performance. Layout-family selection and test sequencing are product hypotheses to validate with ad-library observations and account experiments.
