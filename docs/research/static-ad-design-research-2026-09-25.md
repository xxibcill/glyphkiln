# Static-ad graphic design: evidence and template recommendation

**Research date:** 25 September 2026.  
**Decision:** Start with **six creative families**, **three core social shapes**, and **three distinct concepts in an initial test**. These are separate counts. Six is a manageable product scope, not a scientifically established conversion optimum.

This research prioritizes product/e-commerce ads on Meta and Instagram, with a software comparison and first-party specifications for other channels. It supplements the [graphic-design roadmap](../plans/graphic-design-capability-roadmap.md) and the implemented [Product + Offer template](../product-offer-template.md). It does not qualify the entire advertising family or establish campaign performance.

## 1. What was actually researched

Two evidence streams informed the recommendation:

- **Visual inspection:** The public Meta Ad Library, United States, active ads, advertiser-specific pages, with “Images and memes” selected. The ledger below records 12 inspected static ad cards across The Ridge, Huel US, and Notion. Some cards are closely related or duplicate compositions; these are not 12 independent design concepts. Additional cards were screened but are not used as evidence here.
- **Platform requirements:** Current first-party Google, LinkedIn, Pinterest, TikTok and Meta Blueprint material. Exact specifications, source links, differences between documents and access limitations are in the [platform research note](static-ad-platform-specs-2026-09-25.md).

The sample was purposive: accessible early results from three known advertisers, chosen to compare physical goods, nutrition products and software. It is not random, representative of all industries, or a ranking of successful campaigns. The library's default ordering was retained; no causal inference is drawn from order, duplicate count, launch date or active status. All observations are of the preview variant displayed at inspection time. Several cards explicitly have multiple versions, so their images may change on revisit.

**Important search correction:** A keyword search for Huel initially returned unrelated advertisers. Those results were excluded. Advertiser identity was checked against `ridge`, `huelusa` and `NotionHQ`. Meta's “Images” filter explicitly means images with little or no text; “Memes” means images with text. Using **Images and memes** avoids systematically omitting designed typography. Even that filter returned grouped video previews for Notion; visible video cards were excluded.

No Google Ads Transparency Center or LinkedIn Ad Library creatives are claimed as visually reviewed. Specifications for those channels are document research. Browser screenshots were inspected during this session; this report retains source links and observation notes, not a permanent image archive.

## 3. Six creative families to build

The families answer different buying questions. They are a planning taxonomy, not mutually exclusive labels: one ad can combine an offer, product and testimonial. Assign its primary family by what dominates the reading order.

| Priority / family           | Buying question                               | Layout contract                                                                                                                                                                      | Evidence / confidence                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Product hero             | What is it, and why does it appeal to me?     | One dominant product or use-context image; optional short headline; optional brand mark; optional external/platform CTA. Support complete-product and intentional-detail treatments. | Directly observed in R1–R4. Strong evidence that these compositions exist; no performance claim.                                                              |
| 2. Offer / bundle           | What do I get, at what value?                 | Offer headline or price; single product or assortment; benefit/value support; terms; optional CTA. No invented discount or false urgency.                                            | Directly observed in H2–H3 and N1. Existing Product + Offer is the starting point, but lacks a dedicated assortment/value structure.                          |
| 3. Benefit / feature proof  | What useful result or capability do I get?    | One outcome headline; product or simplified UI; up to three short fact/feature modules; optional annotations or connectors.                                                          | H6 and N1 support this direction. Limit of three modules is our readability heuristic.                                                                        |
| 4. Testimonial / evidence   | Why should I believe it?                      | Dominant quote or evidence; source/attribution; relevant product or person; optional rating only with supplied provenance; disclosure/source space.                                  | H1/H5 demonstrate proof-first hierarchy. Their specific claims were not validated and must not become template defaults.                                      |
| 5. Problem → solution       | How does this solve my situation?             | One recognizable situation or objection, a concrete resolution and one visual demonstration; optionally a two-part layout.                                                           | Huel captions supply problem/solution angles; H4 supplies an informal annotated-photo treatment. A dedicated split-layout execution is an inference to test.  |
| 6. Comparison / alternative | Why choose this instead of my current option? | Two clearly labeled alternatives, two or three comparable attributes, consistent units/basis, one conclusion and source/qualification space.                                         | Proposed extension, not a visually validated two-column pattern in this sample. Prioritize later until a category-specific brief and supporting claims exist. |

**Build next:** Benefit / Feature Proof, then Testimonial / Evidence. These add more expressive range than another product-poster skin. Refine Product Hero and Offer as capabilities of the existing template where appropriate; six creative families do not require six unrelated renderers. Problem/Solution and Comparison follow once their briefs and evidence are ready.

For the first implementation benchmark, use the same fictional product, admitted assets and factual brief across Product + Offer, Benefit Proof and Testimonial/Evidence. Any fictional quote must be clearly labeled as sample content. This isolates whether Glyphkiln can produce meaningfully different hierarchies without changing the brand or inventing factual support.

## 4. How many export formats?

**For the initial social-ad workflow: three shape families.** Make 4:5 the default design workspace for the proposed feed-focused brief, then compose a square and a tall version. This is a production decision, not a claim that 4:5 wins every auction. Do not qualify a platform profile from aspect ratio alone.

| Tier              | Shape  | Proposed master / platform example                                       | Why retain it                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Core              | 4:5    | 1080 × 1350 social master                                                | Feed-oriented portrait composition. Google Demand Gen separately recommends 960 × 1200.                                                        |
| Core              | 1:1    | 1200 × 1200 cross-platform master; existing social output is 1080 × 1080 | Square delivery is documented by Google and LinkedIn; useful fallback composition.                                                             |
| Core              | 9:16   | 1080 × 1920 master                                                       | Tall placements need their own reading order and safe-zone proof. Google Demand Gen lists this shape for Shorts.                               |
| Channel extension | 1.91:1 | 1200 × 628                                                               | Google/LinkedIn landscape. Existing Glyphkiln `linkedin-landscape` is 1200 × 627; close dimensions are not an exact platform-qualified preset. |
| Channel extension | 2:3    | 1000 × 1500                                                              | Pinterest's recommended standard-image shape. Add when Pinterest is in scope.                                                                  |

Sources: [Google Demand Gen creative specifications](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13704860?hl=en), [LinkedIn single-image specifications](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a426534/single-image-ads-advertising-specifications?lang=en-us), [Pinterest specifications](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs).

The Meta Ads Guide pages were login/block-limited during research, so these are proposed social masters, **not freshly verified Meta minimum-size or delivery guarantees**. Verify actual objective/placement acceptance and overlays in Ads Manager before qualification. LinkedIn vertical paid ads are mobile-only, which is a reason to retain square/landscape when desktop matters. Source and limitations are detailed in the companion note.

Fixed-pixel display banners need a separate family with their own compositions and byte budgets. A 728 × 90 leaderboard is not a resized portrait. TikTok's verified standard carousel is a multi-image sequence with required music, not equivalent to a single static poster. Do not count a YouTube thumbnail or organic-carousel preset as ad support solely because the dimensions look suitable.

## 5. Design rules that follow from the evidence

1. **Choose one lead message.** Product, benefit, offer or proof should win the first glance. Optional fields must truly disappear when absent. R1 is a useful counterexample to making every image a poster.
2. **Art-direct the image for the composition.** A wide lifestyle photo inside a tall contain frame may leave the product small. Accept appropriate portrait assets, transparent product cutouts or an explicit detail crop. Preserve supplied assets; do not silently crop a complete-product brief.
3. **Separate the headline from supporting facts.** A short dominant headline and a small group of proof points is a reusable structure in H2/H6/N1. Do not make all text equally prominent.
4. **Review at actual feed size.** Inspect at roughly 360 CSS-pixel width as a design check, as well as full resolution. This is a local readability heuristic, not a platform rule. Tiny labels visible only in a 1080-pixel proof are not sufficient.
5. **Recompose for each shape.** Change image proportions, column/stack logic and optional detail density. Keep claim qualifiers attached to their claim; do not reduce them to fit a badge.
6. **Make treatment diversity intentional.** Test a studio product hero against an informal use-context photo or proof-led composition. Distinguish that from a SKU swap, background recolor or changed export size.
7. **Match the export to the ad system.** Google responsive display combines separate image, logo and text assets and advises against overlaid text/logos, collage images and button graphics. Supply a clean photographic asset path separately from the finished social poster. Do not generalize this guidance into a ban on all social-ad typography. [Google RDA guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9823397?hl=en)

The current Product + Offer proof establishes reproducible output and basic layout adaptation. It has not established that its headline/image balance, CTA treatment or performance is optimal. Its landscape photo is especially worth replacing with aspect-appropriate source material when evaluating tall product prominence.

## 6. How many ads should a first test contain?

**Working recommendation: three distinct concepts, each adapted to required shapes.** If all three core shapes are needed, that is **3 concepts × 3 shapes = 9 files**, not nine independent creative ideas. With enough traffic and budget, two executions per concept produces six creative executions and 18 files. This is an example production matrix, not a minimum spend recommendation or a universal experiment size.

Meta Blueprint advocates diversification across formats, visuals and messages without prescribing a universal static-template count. Google's RDA guide suggests 3–4 ads per ad group; LinkedIn suggests 4–5 variations per campaign. These are platform-specific operating suggestions, not proof that more simultaneous ads always improve outcomes. [Meta Blueprint](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/707402), [Google guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9823397?hl=en), [LinkedIn campaign guide](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/how-to-advertise-on-linkedin)

For a product brief, the initial three hypotheses could be:

- **Desire:** Product Hero / Offer — people respond to the product and its value.
- **Utility:** Benefit Proof — people need to understand a practical advantage.
- **Trust:** Testimonial / Evidence — people need credible reassurance.

Before launch, define the business metric, conversion event, acceptable cost, attribution window and comparison method. For sales, purchase economics are the primary outcome; clicks are diagnostic. For lead generation, include lead quality, not just form volume. Use a controlled experiment when claiming a causal lift. Ordinary algorithmic delivery gives unequal exposure, so raw winner/loser labels need caution. If traffic cannot support three useful comparisons, test fewer concepts sequentially and document the time-period limitation; there is no evidence-based fixed “three-day winner” rule here.

After the concept test, change one material element at a time when the question is about that element: headline, image, proof, offer or CTA. Keep a record of the concept, execution and export shape separately. This is the way to learn which design mechanism helped rather than merely accumulate asset files.

| 4    | Placement-aware export profiles                                                                       | Exact dimensions, safe-zone evidence, file-type/byte checks, clean responsive assets distinct from complete artwork, verified actual campaign previews. |
| 5    | Concept / execution / export manifest grouping                                                        | Resizes do not inflate concept count; exact template/version/assets/copy preserved; learning can map back to reproducible files.                        |
| 6    | Problem/Solution and Comparison                                                                       | Category-specific references and substantiated brief before claiming these families qualified.                                                          |

Any implementation must retain deterministic rendering, treat content as data, version deliberate pixel changes and include pixel-affecting regression tests. Complete App authoring/save/reopen/revise/export paths and reviewed artifacts are required before calling a family ready. This research changes no renderer, schema, pixels or baselines.

## 8. Confidence and unresolved questions

- **High confidence:** What was visible in the listed ad previews; exact wording of the linked platform documents at research time; the need to separate concept count, shape count and experiment count.
- **Moderate confidence:** Six families provide a useful initial library for these product/software use cases. This is a bounded product-design recommendation based on observed patterns and coverage gaps.
- **Unestablished:** Which family has the highest conversion rate for the user's product; whether six is globally optimal; account-specific placement acceptance; performance benefit of any particular template.
- **Research gaps:** Category-specific competitors, local-language/Thai-market creatives, a wider sample of small advertisers, direct Google/LinkedIn creative inspection, a validated comparison example, permanent visual evidence capture, actual campaign results.

The next useful input is a real product brief, audience, offer and campaign objective. The next useful implementation is Benefit / Feature Proof. Neither requires pretending that a public ad library can identify the best-performing ad.
